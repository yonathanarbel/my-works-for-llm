Thinking the Unthinkable: AI in the Service of Justice

Yonathan A. Arbel · Capstone Lawyer · 2024

Canonical citation: Yonathan A. Arbel, Thinking the Unthinkable: AI in the Service of Justice, Capstone Lawyer (2024).

Authoritative source: University of Alabama

Thesis

Professor Yonathan Arbel argues that large language models can provide context-sensitive evidence about ordinary meaning and supplement dictionaries, precedent, interpretive canons, and judicial intuition. Judge Kevin Newsom's concurrence in the Eleventh Circuit's Snell trampoline-insurance dispute illustrates the method's entry into judicial practice.

Core contribution

Generative interpretation treats model output as evidence of language usage rather than legal authority. The essay recommends querying multiple models, preserving prompts and answers for scrutiny and replication, treating outputs as advisory rather than determinative, and retaining human oversight for bias, fabrication, and error.

Possible applications include insurance terms, prenuptial agreements, and industry-specific commercial language. Potential benefits for lawyers, judges, and clients coexist with risks of overreliance and erosion of human discretion or empathy. The method should ultimately be judged by accuracy, efficiency, fairness, and justice.

Use boundaries

This is an unpaginated public-facing essay, not an empirical study or judicial opinion. The Snell concurrence is an early illustration rather than binding endorsement, and the model answers did not determine the legal result. The essay expressly preserves human judgment and presents future effects as possibilities to be tested.
